# Local demo data v1

Demo data is explicit, deterministic, tenant-scoped, versioned, idempotent, and local-development only. Production routes contain no demo fallback.

`npm run pilot:setup:demo` creates four suppliers, six items, three customers, one demo warehouse, three locations, two payment terms, and two tax codes. Stable identifiers begin with `LOCAL-DEMO-`. A collision with a non-demo supplier code or item SKU fails instead of overwriting the record.

`npm run pilot:setup:scenario` requires `pilot:setup` (for workspace roles), the master demo, and a USD workspace. It creates the US walkthrough scenario: one open purchase request, one RFQ, 32 purchase orders from six suppliers across every status (draft, pending approval, approved, issued, partially received, fully received, cancelled), 14 receiving documents with arrival times (early, on-time, late and rejected deliveries), 11 supplier invoices due across the current, 1-30, 31-60, 61-90 and 90+ aging buckets, and one sales order. Every date is an offset from the seed day in the workspace timezone; pass `-- --as-of=YYYY-MM-DD` (or set `FLOWCHAIN_SCENARIO_AS_OF`) to pin it. Invoice variances are stored as a three-way match computes them, tax-exclusive, with the `price_variance` type code. Invoices use committed statuses (submitted, matched, approved, and LOCAL-DEMO-INV-001 as the one exception, with its price variance); the seeded approved status and date do not create payable obligations. Receipts are posted through the receiving posting command service as of their arrival, so purchase order received quantities, receipt status and inventory movements agree; accepted quantities add to stock and rejected quantities stay on the receipt. Posted receipts are inventory history, so re-running the command keeps the first seed day, never posts a receipt twice, and refuses a different `--as-of`; seed a fresh database to change the day. Documents a business command has changed (their version has moved) are left as they are. These records are marked `localDemo` and do not create payables, payments, or journal entries.

Since scenario version 8 the walkthrough also has a shipment history, so the reorder list computes demand: 60 customer orders, one on each weekday of the 12 weeks before the seed day (days -84 to -1, so the reorder list's 90 days still hold all of them up to six days after seeding), with quantities drawn from one fixed random seed (the same orders for every seed day), each shipped in full that afternoon. Each item has its own demand shape: LDM-002 smooth, LDM-001 regular, LDM-004 lumpy, LDM-006 intermittent. Stock starts with one opening stock entry (`LOCAL-DEMO-ADJ-OPENING`, reason opening balance) on the day the workspace went live, 160 days before the seed day, onto one stock record per item (`LOCAL-DEMO-BAL-001` to `-006`); the orders are reserved, drafted and posted through the outbound commands, in date order with the receipts, so stock never goes below zero and on-hand quantities at the seed day are what they were before (LDM-001 still 28). Each item's safety stock and reorder point are the same in its columns, its metadata and its stock record; LDM-001 keeps 30 / 40, the others are about daily demand times lead time plus safety stock. The six items' preferred sources get item-supplier links with a lead time, a minimum order quantity and a reference price. On the seed day the reorder list orders LDM-002 now: PO-002 and PO-021 are due after its order-by day and left out, and its 45 short are raised to Acme's minimum order of 50. It stays so for six days after seeding, with PO-021 left out throughout (PO-002 counts once it is due). Running `pilot:setup:scenario` again on a workspace seeded before version 8 keeps its stock and adds only the thresholds and supplier links (`walkthrough:local` seeds only on its first run); start the walkthrough with `--reset` for the history. No customer invoices are created for the shipped orders.

`node scripts/export-replenishment-inputs.mjs` writes, as JSON and in one read-only transaction, what a replenishment optimizer needs for each active item: on hand and reserved, the open purchase order lines with their due days and prices, recorded reorder point and safety stock, the preferred supplier's lead time, minimum order quantity and prices, the 90 daily shipped quantities, and the reorder list's own row with its request quantity (`qRule`), plus the walkthrough's seed day and random seed. `--walkthrough-dir=<folder> --pg-port=<port>` reads a local walkthrough; `--out=<file>` writes a file.

Running either command repeatedly leaves the same record counts. Ordinary `pilot:setup` creates no business demo records.
